Proposal · Gabriel for Denver

The Platform, Written Ahead

Build the Field Guide on two brand-new content collections at a brand-new route, so the whole platform can be written months before anyone sees it — without a single edit to the pages currently deployed.

Grounded against main @ 07970fe — 13 Sept 2026  ·  design.html  ·  schema.html

01

Summary

Direction B from Issues at Two Speeds becomes the campaign's policy surface: one promise per full-bleed band, a giant statistic or pull-quote as the hero, and the policy detail in a sheet. It is backed by two new CMS collections — guides (one per issue area) and planks (one per promise) — living in their own folders, edited in their own place in Sveltia, and rendered at a new /platform route. Nothing in src/content/issues/ or at /issues changes, which is what makes it safe to draft the entire platform in public-facing tooling while the live site carries on unaltered. Three PRs: the content model first so writing can start immediately, then the pages, then a cutover that is gated on the writing actually being done rather than on any code.

02

Chosen approach

Direction B is the brief, so the decision this proposal actually makes is how to build B such that the three costs its own critique named don't land. All three turn out to be cheap to close.

1. The sheet is a <details>, not an overlay

B's stated weakness was that detail in a side sheet “reads as an afterthought and is harder to link and to print.” That is true of a JavaScript overlay and false of a server-rendered <details> element that JavaScript merely promotes into a fixed panel. The prose is in the HTML either way, so it prints, it is crawled, it feeds the meta description, and a direct hit on #plank-<slug> opens it natively with JS off. This is the standard progressive-enhancement posture for interactive components — core content server-rendered, the History API carrying state into a real URL — and it costs roughly forty lines.

2. The number is the art

B's other cost was “a real illustration or photo per band, so it has an ongoing art cost.” The answer is to put no art on the bands at all. The hero is the typography: a 13rem numeral in whichever brand colour is neither the band's ground nor its text — yellow on the blue band, blue on the pale band, navy on the gold band — with nothing else competing with it, which is precisely what makes one band work as a screenshot. The marginal art cost of a new plank is not reduced but eliminated. The six existing illustrations keep their full size on the guide hero and on the index cards, at the scale they were actually drawn for (Q6).

3. Length is managed, not pretended away

One band per plank does mean twelve planks is twelve screens, and no design amendment changes that. What helps is giving the reader somewhere to stand: a pinned rail down the left with one tick per plank, and a /platform index that lists every commitment headline across all six guides — Direction C's idea, adopted where its value actually was. That index is also the thing you hand an endorsement questionnaire.

4. Two parallel collections, not an extension of issues

This is the part the brief turns on. Sveltia is configured against backend.branch: main with no editorial workflow, so every save in the CMS is a commit to main and a production deploy. “Create the content ahead of time without ruining deployed content” therefore cannot be achieved by discipline; it has to be structural. Separate collections in separate folders read by separate routes means a half-finished draft is physically incapable of appearing on /issues, because nothing on that page reads those files. It is the expand-and-contract shape: build the new surface alongside the old, run both, switch traffic, retire the old one — with the retirement deliberately deferred to its own change so a revert is one commit.

Visibility before launch copies a pattern the repo already runs rather than inventing one: posts is built, reachable by URL, marked noindex via the layout prop that already exists, excluded from the sitemap in astro.config.mjs, and linked from no navigation. Draft guides get exactly that treatment, which means Gabriel can open his own draft on the real domain and text the link to a comms volunteer without any of it being discoverable.

5. Spanish is gated at publish, not at save

Every _es field on issues is required today, which guarantees the two language sites never diverge and also guarantees nothing can be saved half-written. The new collections keep the guarantee and drop the cost by moving the gate: _es is optional in Zod, so a plank can be drafted in English and saved, and completeness is enforced in the build instead — an entry with status: published missing any _es twin fails the build, naming the entry and the fields. Drafting stays fluid, and a half-translated plank cannot reach either live site (Q3).

6. Logic in src/lib/, because that is what this repo can test

Vitest is configured for src/**/*.test.ts in a node environment; there is no Astro component testing and no way to add it cheaply. The repo has already solved this once — src/lib/events.ts holds the “which events are still upcoming” filter precisely so it can be tested, with the .astro files kept as thin templates. Band-theme rotation, hero resolution, draft filtering, the guide↔plank join and its assertions all go into src/lib/platform.ts for the same reason.

That module is also where the two publish-time assertions live — the guide↔plank join and the translation gate — so both are covered by ordinary unit tests rather than being discovered by a red build.

One deliberate deviation from repo convention: the band and sheet components take a lang prop rather than shipping as an index.astro/es.astro pair. That convention has already drifted in the surface being replaced — the Spanish issue page has no analytics at all, and its index anchor is #retos while every back-link points at #what-will-i-tackle. Duplicating markup that is a near-clone is how that happens, and this is a good place to stop doing it.

03

Alternatives considered

Extend issues with optional fields rejected

The original artifact's own recommendation: additive fields on the existing collection, six files migrated one at a time, no new route. Genuinely the smaller change.

Why not: it fails the brief directly. A CMS save commits to main, so an editor drafting a commitment is editing the very file /issues/housing-crisis renders. Worse, every _es field on issues is required, so a plank cannot even be saved until it is fully translated. And the “Issues” form in Sveltia would grow roughly fourteen fields that do nothing on any deployed page — a form that lies to the person filling it in.

One new collection, planks as a nested list widget rejected

Mirror the existing shape: one file per guide with a planks: list inside it. No join, no reference, no spike, half the CMS config.

Why not: draft status collapses to per-guide, so there is no way to publish a guide with three planks ready and two still being written — which is the exact workflow this is for. It also recreates the doubly-nested Sveltia list widget (subIssues[].problemsAndSolutions[]) that makes today's content unpleasant to add to, and it means every plank edit rewrites a large file, so two people cannot work on one guide at once.

Sveltia editorial workflow rejected for now

publish_mode: editorial_workflow puts every unpublished entry on its own branch with a pull request, which solves the isolation problem at the CMS layer rather than the content-model layer.

Why not: it changes the publishing model for every collection, including Events, which the campaign updates weekly and under time pressure — a volunteer adding a canvass event should not have to understand a pull request. It is also the more invasive change to a system that currently works. Worth revisiting once the campaign has more than one regular editor; kept as F5.

A separate preview deploy for drafts rejected

Keep drafts out of production entirely and view them on a Netlify branch deploy or a second site.

Why not: the CMS commits to main. Pointing it at a draft branch instead means someone has to merge that branch by hand to publish anything, which is more infrastructure and more human steps than a campaign has capacity for — and it takes away the thing that actually matters, which is being able to look at the real page on the real domain.

One page per plank rejected

/platform/<guide>/<plank> as real routes — the strongest possible linkability, and the friendliest thing to hand a journalist.

Why not: eighteen to forty pages of two paragraphs each, none of them substantial enough to rank or to share on their own, and it dissolves the uninterrupted scroll that is the entire argument for Direction B. The #plank-<slug> anchor gets most of the linkability at none of the cost.

Design

Mobile and desktop mockups at real dimensions, scrollable, including the sheet-open state on both and the /platform index: design.html.

Two decisions in there worth flagging because they are not obvious from looking: the band's two-column desktop layout is driven by a container query, not a viewport media query, so the same component renders correctly inside the narrower column of a future index or embed; and the sheet is deliberately white on every band theme rather than inheriting the band's palette, because it is the one surface on the page someone actually reads at length.

07

Open questions & risks

All six answered in chat on 13 Sept 2026, three of them against the recommendation. They stay listed with their recommendations and alternatives intact — that is the record of what was weighed, not something to rewrite once a decision lands.

Q1: Should draft guides build on the live site as unlisted noindex pages, or be excluded from the production build entirely? ✓ ANSWERED

The CMS commits to main, so the only build that ever runs is the production one — there is no staging environment and no dev server Gabriel is going to run. If drafts are excluded from the production build, there is no way for anyone to look at the new platform until the moment it goes live, which makes writing it a blind exercise. If they are built and merely unlisted, they are real URLs on the real domain: crawl-suppressed, sitemap-suppressed, linked from nowhere, but guessable and shareable. There is no third option that does not involve standing up infrastructure.

My Recommendation
Build them, unlisted. This is not a new pattern — posts already works exactly this way, using the noindex prop that layouts/default.astro already accepts and a one-line sitemap exclusion that astro.config.mjs already has the shape for. The content is a draft of a public political platform, not anything confidential, and the ability to open the real page on the real domain and send the link to someone is worth more to this campaign than protection against a URL guess. One honest wrinkle: the sitemap filter only sees a URL, not content, so the exclusion has to cover the whole /platform tree until cutover. After cutover, any guide still in draft will appear in sitemap.xml while carrying noindex — Google honours the tag and files it as “Excluded by noindex,” which is untidy rather than harmful. F6 fixes it properly if drafts-after-cutover become routine.
Alternatives to Consider
Building drafts only when an INCLUDE_DRAFTS environment variable is set, and configuring a second Netlify context with it on. Genuinely airtight, and cheap if the campaign is willing to hold two deploy contexts in its head — but it is a piece of infrastructure nobody has asked for, and it hands Gabriel a second domain to remember.
Answer
Building drafts unlisted, per the recommendation — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. PR 2 ships the noindex prop and the sitemap exclusion. The wrinkle flagged above does not survive to production after all: F6 was brought into scope and lands in PR 3, so the sitemap filter consults the actual draft slugs instead of excluding the whole tree.

Q2: Is /platform the permanent URL, or should the guides move back to /issues once the old pages are retired? ✓ ANSWERED

A new route is what makes the parallel build safe, but it is not obviously the right long-term address. /issues has been shared for a year, it is what the current nav says, and inbound links point at it. /platform is a better description of what the page now is — a set of promises, not a set of problems — and it is easier to say out loud. Whichever loses needs a permanent redirect, and doing that twice would be worse than doing it once.

My Recommendation
Keep /platform permanently and 301 /issues/* to it in PR 3. The redirect map is small and mechanical (the guide slugs deliberately match the issue slugs), astro.config.mjs already carries a redirects block from the donate-path moves, and the word is simply more accurate — a voter looking for what a candidate will actually do is not looking for “issues.”
Alternatives to Consider
Building at /platform and swapping the guides back onto /issues at cutover, with /platform redirecting the other way. Keeps every existing link and every printed piece of literature valid with no redirect at all, at the cost of a URL that undersells the page. Worth taking seriously if any physical material already carries the /issues address.
Answer
/platform is the permanent URL, per the recommendation — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. PR 3 carries the 301s for /issues/* and /es/issues/*, with a test asserting every old slug resolves to a real guide.

Q3: Must a plank be translated before it can publish, or should the Spanish pages simply skip untranslated planks? ✓ ANSWERED

Today every _es field on issues is required, which guarantees the two languages never diverge and also guarantees nothing can be saved half-done. The new collections make _es optional so drafting is possible at all — but that reopens the question of what /es/platform/housing-crisis renders when plank three has no Spanish. Falling back to English inside a Spanish page is the one answer that is clearly wrong.

My Recommendation
Skip untranslated planks on the Spanish routes, and surface the gap in the CMS rather than on the page — a summary template on the Planks collection that marks an entry as English-only, so the backlog is visible to whoever is doing the translating. A Spanish reader then sees a shorter but entirely coherent guide, which is far better than a bilingual one, and the English page is never held hostage to translation capacity.
Alternatives to Consider
Requiring _es before status can be set to published, enforced in the build rather than the schema so drafting still works. Stronger guarantee, and it keeps the two sites genuinely equal — the right call if Spanish-language voters are a priority audience rather than a courtesy, which is a campaign question and not a technical one.
Answer
Taking the alternative — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. _es stays optional in Zod so drafts can be saved in English, and completeness is enforced at publish time in the build: a published guide or plank missing any _es twin fails, naming the entry and the fields. The Spanish routes therefore never skip a plank, because a half-translated one cannot be published in the first place. Written up as approach 5, and tested in PR 1 as assertTranslated().

Q4: Is every number going to get a real source, and what renders on the bands that don't? ✓ ANSWERED

The whole format rests on an enormous figure being trustworthy. A 13rem 77% with no citation under it is a much bigger claim than the same number buried in a paragraph, and an opponent only has to find one unsourced figure. The existing content has the numbers (45%, 28%, 77%, ~10%) and no URLs for any of them. Partial sourcing is the worst outcome — cited numbers next to bare ones reads as evasion about the bare ones.

My Recommendation
Treat a source as a hard requirement for a stat band specifically, and lean on the pull-quote and statement bands for everything else. The schema deliberately supports three hero kinds so this is a real option rather than a compromise: a plank with no defensible figure gets a pull-quote or its own commitment as the hero and loses nothing. That way every number on the site is cited, without the sourcing burden dictating how many bands exist.
Alternatives to Consider
Shipping numbers uncited for now and adding footnotes as they are found. Faster to a complete-looking platform, and the schema makes backfilling a source a one-field edit — the risk is that “for now” survives into October.
Answer
Taking the alternative — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. Numbers ship uncited and citations are backfilled as they are found. sources stays optional and a band with none renders without its footnote line, which design.html now draws explicitly on the gold band rather than showing only the happy path. The obligation this defers is tracked as F7, because “for now” is exactly the kind of thing that survives into October unless it is written down.

Q5: How much of the policy detail should sit in the sheet rather than on the band? ✓ ANSWERED

The mockups put the commitment, the why line, the timeline chip and the source on the band, and everything else — the problem prose, the authority note, the full source list — behind the sheet. Push more onto the band and the format loses the density that makes it shareable; push less and the band becomes a slogan with a link under it. The why line is the hinge: it is what keeps a closed sheet from hiding the argument.

My Recommendation
As drawn, with the why line kept mandatory in the schema. It is the smallest amount of reasoning that makes a band self-contained, so a screenshot of one band is an argument rather than an assertion — and the sheet then genuinely is supporting material rather than the missing half of the page.
Alternatives to Consider
Promoting authority onto the band as a second always-visible line. “A Council vote, no ballot measure” is the single most distinguishing thing a city-council candidate can say — most platforms promise things the office cannot deliver — and it may be worth the vertical space on the bands where it is strongest.
Answer
As drawn, per the recommendation — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. why stays mandatory in the schema and authority stays in the sheet.

Q6: Do the six illustrations survive as 8% watermarks, or should the bands be purely typographic? ✓ ANSWERED

The existing SVGs are distinctive and they are doing real brand work on the current pages, where each one gets a half-hero. In the Field Guide they are reduced to a tinted watermark bleeding off the band edge, which is a large demotion for artwork someone commissioned. The alternative is bands that are pure type and colour, with the illustrations moved to the /platform index as the guide markers.

My Recommendation
Keep them as watermarks and also use them at full size on the index cards. At 8% they add texture and a hint of subject without competing with the numeral, which is the one thing on the band that must win — and the index is where they get to be themselves again, at the size they were drawn for.
Alternatives to Consider
Dropping them from the bands entirely. A band of nothing but a 13rem numeral on a flat brand colour is a stronger, more confident poster, and it is closer to how this design language works on Instagram — where it is going to be screenshotted — than anything with a decorative element in the corner.
Answer
Taking the alternative — confirmed in chat on 13 Sept 2026. The bands carry no illustration at all; they are type and colour only. The six SVGs keep their full size on the guide hero and on the index cards. Consequence for the schema: the per-plank artwork, artworkAlt and artworkAlt_es fields have been removed from planks entirely, since with no band artwork there was nothing left for them to override.

Required

F1: Retire the issues collection and its routes

→ now PR 4 of 5

What is this?
Delete src/content/issues/, the issues collection from content.config.ts and from the CMS, and the four /issues page files, leaving only the redirects.
Why do this?
Two content models for the same thing is the state this proposal is deliberately passing through, not living in. Leaving both means a future editor can put a policy promise in the wrong place, and every subsequent change to issue content has to ask which system it belongs to.
Disposition
Brought into scope as its own PR. Writing it up surfaced something this entry had glossed over: what-will-i-tackle renders the issue grid on the home page, so the component has to be repointed at guides before anything can be deleted. That is real work with a real regression risk, and it is the reason the item earns a PR rather than being a tail on the cutover.

F2: Alert on a failed Netlify build

tracked as a task

What is this?
Turn on Netlify's failed-deploy notification to email or Slack, pointed at whoever is on call for the site.
Why do this?
A CMS save is a commit to main, so a content entry that fails validation takes down the entire site's next deploy — not just the page it belongs to. That risk exists today, but this plan raises it: two new collections, forty-odd new entries, non-technical people authoring them, and now two publish-time assertions that can fail. Right now nothing tells anyone; the site just silently stops updating, and the first signal is someone noticing an event never appeared.
Disposition
Stays a follow-up because it is a setting in the Netlify dashboard, not a code change — there is nothing for a PR to contain. Tracked as its own task so it does not evaporate. It wants switching on before PR 1's content starts being edited, not after.

F7: Backfill a citation for every number on the platform

new, from Q4

What is this?
Find and record a source for each statValue shipping without one, and fill in the sources field on those planks.
Why do this?
Q4 chose to ship figures uncited and backfill them, which is a reasonable call about sequencing and a bad end state. A 13rem 77% is a far larger claim than the same number inside a paragraph, and partial sourcing reads worse than none — cited numbers standing next to bare ones invite the question of what is wrong with the bare ones. An opponent has to find exactly one.
Why isn't this in the plan?
It is content work, not code: sources is a one-field edit per plank in the CMS and no PR can contain it. It is listed as Required rather than left implicit precisely because Q4's answer created a debt with no deadline attached, and this is the only place that debt is written down.

Optional

F3: Tag filter and a printable view on /platform

deferred

What is this?
Filter chips on the index (cost of living, housing, safety, tech & privacy, food) and a print stylesheet that renders the whole platform as a document.
Why do this?
An endorsement questionnaire wants one URL containing every promise, and a filtered view is how you answer “what is your housing platform” without sending six links. The print view is what you hand someone across a table.

F4: Read the plank_detail_opened funnel

instrumentation → PR 5 of 5

What is this?
A PostHog view of which planks people actually open, broken down by guide and by language.
Why do this?
The current pages cannot answer “which of these policies do voters care about” at all — issue_viewed is as granular as it gets. Per-plank opens are a genuinely useful signal for what to lead with in canvassing and in email.

F5: Revisit Sveltia's editorial workflow

declined

What is this?
Turn on publish_mode: editorial_workflow so unpublished entries live on their own branch with a pull request, instead of relying on a status field.
Why do this?
It would make the status field redundant and give real review before anything reaches main — which starts to matter the moment more than one person is editing, or a volunteer has CMS access.
Why to not do this?
It applies to every collection, so the person adding next Tuesday's canvass now has a pull request between them and the website. For a one-editor campaign that is a cost with no matching benefit, and the status field covers the actual need at a fraction of the friction.
Disposition
Declined. The status field is the mechanism, full stop. Worth knowing that this decision makes F2 load-bearing rather than merely prudent: with no pull request between an editor and production, a failed build is the only signal that something went in wrong.
