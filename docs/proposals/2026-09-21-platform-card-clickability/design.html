  1. 1
    Arrow chip beside the titleThe one cue that is on screen the moment a card appears, including on a phone where the footer is a screen away. A filled primary circle, so it reads as a control rather than an icon. It is decorative to a screen reader, so the link's name doesn't change for it.
  2. 2
    A footer that says what happensA full-width strip pinned to the bottom of the card, saying "Read the full guide" with an arrow. This is the cue that names the action. It is plain text that sits under the card's link, not a second link, so a tap on it is a tap on the card and the markup stays valid.
  3. 3
    Resting depth and a primary-tinted edgeA soft shadow and a border that is the brand blue at 50%, about 3.4:1 against white instead of today's 1.4:1. Hover deepens the shadow, firms the border to solid primary, nudges both arrows right and darkens the footer.

Every action is its own link

Each row on a card leads straight to that policy, and the rest of the card still leads to the guide. Rows carry a small chevron at rest because a touch screen has no hover to reveal one. It is the same behaviour as the #plank-<slug> permalinks, which already open the policy sheet on arrival.

One pointer, one answer. While a row is hovered, the card behind it does not lift or darken its footer: two things responding to the same pointer reads as noise. Keyboard focus is separate too. Tabbing lands first on the title (a ring around the whole card, as today), then on each row (a ring around that row only). That makes four tab stops per card, 24 across the six guides.

States

Rest, hover, pressed and keyboard focus. On a touch screen there is no hover, so pressed is the one state a finger ever sees; it deepens the footer and settles the card back down so the tap registers.

Why two cues, on a phone

At 375px wide each card is 716 to 740px tall, measured on the running site. When a card scrolls into view the reader sees its top, and the footer is most of a screen further down.

Reached after the artwork and the action list. It is what tells a reader who has read that far what to do next.

The footer is not moved up. Putting a button directly under the stance would put it first on a phone, but it would sit between the words and the artwork and split the card in two. The footer stays where a reader finishes; the chip does the work at the top.

The mini card on /why-me

The condensed topic cards on the "Why me?" page link to the same guides with the same hairline treatment, so they have the same problem. They get the resting depth and a small arrow chip, and their action rows become links like the big card's (Q9). They do not get a footer: their content is already the action list, and a strip would double the height of a compact card.
