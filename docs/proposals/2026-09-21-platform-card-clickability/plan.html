Proposal · Gabriel for Denver

Pressable Guide Cards

The platform cards are handsome, but nothing on them says they lead anywhere. Add an arrow beside the title, a labelled footer and a little depth, so a visitor who has never been told to click one still does.

Grounded against main @ 548fdf4 — 21 Sept 2026, re-checked after review feedback: nothing has merged since  ·  design.html  ·  no schema.html: nothing here touches content or the data model

Summary

Each guide card on /platform, the home page and /issues (and their Spanish twins) is a single link, but it looks like a finished summary: a faint grey hairline, no arrow, no verb, and every action already listed on the face. The only hint is a 4px lift on hover, which needs a pointer and never appears on a phone. This proposal adds three signifiers to the one shared component: a filled arrow chip beside the title, a full-width footer reading “Read the full guide”, and a shadow and brand-tinted border at rest, with hover, pressed and focus states to match. The condensed cards on /why-me get the chip and the depth.

Review added two things. Every action row on a card becomes its own link, straight to that policy's sheet, so the list itself is the second cue that the card is clickable and a visitor who came for one policy skips a step. And a dedicated guide_card_clicked PostHog event records every click with the surface it came from, in place of a before-and-after baseline. Three PRs, in that order: the look, then the links, then the event. No new dependency, no content change, and it covers eight page routes at once.

What the investigation found

One component
guide-card.astro renders every card on /platform, /es/platform and, through what-will-i-tackle, on /, /es, /issues and /es/issues. topic-actions.astro is a smaller copy of the same idea on /why-me and /es/why-me. Those are the only two files using the raised-card classes.
Rest state
border-black/15 composites to #d9d9d9 on white, a contrast of 1.4:1. No shadow, no arrow, no label. Tinted borders and shadows are what tell people a box is an object.
Only cue
hover:-translate-y-1 and active:-translate-y-1. Tailwind v4 emits the hover: variant inside @media (hover:hover) (checked in the built CSS), so on a phone the lift only shows as the finger goes down, after the decision to tap has been made.
Phone size
At 375px wide each card measures 716 to 740px tall (measured on the dev server). A cue at the bottom is most of a screen away when the card first appears.
Already complete
The card lists all of a guide's actions, so it reads as the whole story. The guide page holds the reasoning, the evidence and the timeline, and nothing on the card hints at that.
House idiom
On the guide pages, the control that opens more is a bordered pill ending in an arrow: “Read the policy →” in sheet.astro. The cards are the outlier.
History
#17 made the whole card the link and added the lift; #35 copied that treatment to the /why-me mini cards. The lift was meant as the affordance. It is a good state for a card already known to be clickable, and that is a different job from telling a first-time visitor.
Deep links
Arriving on a guide page at #plank-<slug> already opens that policy's sheet, via the location.hash handling at the bottom of sheet-behavior.astro, and fires plank_detail_opened. A card row can only link to an anchor that exists: cards list the published planks of published guides (selectPublished), and a published guide's page renders exactly its published planks (planksForPage).

Prior art

  • Nielsen Norman Group defines a card as “a snapshot-like display intended to encourage users to click to view more details” (Cards: UI-Component Definition), so a card with nothing that invites the click fails at its own job.
  • NN/g's Beyond Blue Links finds that flat design removed most of the cues people used to read clickability. Its recommendations: keep buttons rectangular with rounded corners, make an image, its text and its icon one target, and don't lean on an arrow alone, which it calls a less effective cue than a label.
  • Card accessibility guidance recommends layering cues: a chevron or arrow, a visible action phrase, and hover states, none of them alone (Berkeley's accessible card patterns; the same conclusion appears in most of the card-design write-ups I found).
  • Heydon Pickering's Inclusive Components: Cards puts the link on the heading and stretches it over the whole card with a pseudo-element, so other links inside the card can sit above it. That pattern is what makes per-row links possible, and it is adopted here. Its stated cost is that the overlay complicates selecting the card's text.

Chosen approach

Three signifiers in one component, each covering a case the others miss, then two changes from review: rows that link, and a click event.

1. An arrow chip at the end of the title row

A 36px filled primary circle holding a right arrow (ArrowRight from @lucide/astro, already a dependency). It is the one cue on screen the instant a card appears, including on the 720px phone cards. It is aria-hidden, so it changes nothing for a screen reader.

2. A footer that names the action

A full-width strip pinned to the card's bottom edge with mt-auto, so the strips line up across a row of unequal cards. It reads “Read the full guide →”, in solid primary (Q1). This is the cue NN/g says an arrow can't be: a verb. It is plain text under the card's link overlay (see 4), so a tap on it is a tap on the card, and it is aria-hidden because the link's accessible name already contains it (Q3).

3. Depth and a tinted edge at rest, with a full state set

The border changes from black at 15% (1.4:1) to primary at 50% (3.4:1), and a soft primary-tinted shadow lifts the card off the page. Hover keeps the existing 4px lift and adds a deeper shadow, a solid primary border, both arrows sliding right and the footer darkening to tertiary. Pressed settles the card back and darkens the footer, which is the one state a touch user sees. Reduced-motion users get the colour and shadow changes without the movement, which the card doesn't handle today.

4. Each action row is its own link into that policy

The card's markup changes from one <a> around everything to an <article> with the stretched-link pattern. The title is a heading link to the guide, carrying aria-label="Title, Read the full guide", with an ::after that covers the whole card. Each row is a real link at a higher z-index, pointing at plankUrl(guideHref, slug), the helper that already builds #plank-<slug> permalinks. Arriving there opens the policy's sheet (Q7).

Rows are visibly links: a chevron at the end of each at rest (Q8), and a tint and underline on hover. While a row is hovered the card behind it doesn't lift, done with :has() in the shared utility, because two things responding to one pointer reads as noise. Keyboard focus is separate too: a ring around the whole card on the title, a ring around one row on a row. The /why-me mini cards get the same structure (Q9). Costs accepted: four tab stops per card (24 across six guides, up from six), and the overlay makes the card's own text hard to select. A card that is one big link already behaves much the same.

5. One click event with the surface on it

guide_card_clicked carries surface (platform, home, issues or why-me), lang, guide_slug and target (card or action), plus plank_slug on an action. Surface and language together separate the six guide-card routes (three surfaces in two languages) from each other, and the two /why-me routes make eight (Q10). The same component appears on / and /issues, so the surface can't be a prop; it comes from the URL through a small pure function. One delegated listener handles every card on a page. The event stays consistent with what the destination already reports: the guide page fires platform_guide_viewed and, after a row click, plank_detail_opened, so a click and its arrival can be joined in PostHog.

Why this rather than something smaller or bigger

The footer is the piece doing the work the current card lacks: it says what happens. The chip is there because the footer is off-screen on a phone when the card first appears. Depth is there because the footer alone still leaves a card that looks flat until read to the bottom. Rows that link give the list itself a job, so a card that was a dead-end summary becomes a menu of policies. None of it needs a dependency or a content change, and nothing new runs on the server. The real cost is review effort, which is why it is split into three PRs.

Shared styling and where the logic goes

The raised-card class string is currently duplicated in guide-card.astro and topic-actions.astro with a comment saying they match. PR 1 moves it to one @utility card-link in global.css, following the existing @utility btn, so the two can't drift again. Astro components can't be tested here (see AGENTS.md), so copy and logic go into pure functions with tests beside them: guideCardCta(lang) and guideCardLabel(title, lang) in src/lib/platform.ts next to actionCount; cardSurface(pathname) and cardClickProps(link, lang) in a new src/lib/card-tracking.ts, tested under jsdom the way sheet.ts is. global.test.ts already compiles the stylesheet to check one utility; it gets cases checking that the card's hover styles sit inside @media (hover:hover), so a touch device can never be left with a stuck raised card.

Alternatives considered

Strengthen the hover and shadow only

Not chosen

Cheapest option: a bigger lift and a shadow. It is a hidden affordance, invisible until a pointer is already over the card, and it does nothing on touch, where the reporter's confusion is most likely to happen. Depth is kept as one of the three cues, not the lead.

An arrow icon alone

Kept as part of the chosen approach

The smallest change, and NN/g's own findings list arrows among the weaker cues when used alone. As a chip beside the title, it earns its place by being the cue visible at the top of a tall card.

Underlined, coloured title

Not chosen

The classic link signifier. On a 30px semibold title, repeated across six cards, it adds noise to a grid that is otherwise calm, and it leaves the lower two thirds of the card looking inert. It also competes with the chip for the same job.

Footer button only

Not chosen alone

Names the action but sits 700px down on a phone. Kept, paired with the chip.

Expand the card in place

Not chosen

An accordion matches the word “expandable”, but a guide is a whole page: a hero, one band per action, statistics, sources and a share sheet. Folding that into a card loses the URL people share and the platform_guide_viewed event, and gives up the print and no-JS behaviour the field-guide proposal built on purpose.

A first-visit coach mark

Not chosen

“Tap a card to read more” needs JavaScript and stored state, is missed by anyone who doesn't see it first, and teaches the page instead of fixing it.

Make each action row its own link into that policy

Chosen in review (was follow-up F2)

First listed as out of scope: a tap on “Legalize congregational housing” opens that policy's sheet directly, which #plank-<slug> already does natively. It can't be done inside a single <a>, so it means the stretched-link restructure, and it adds tab stops. It was brought into scope anyway. It doesn't by itself tell a reader that the card as a whole is clickable, which is why it ships after the signifiers rather than instead of them.

Design

See design.html for before and after cards with real copy, the numbered signifiers, the hit map and row states for the per-row links, all four card states, the solid and tinted footer, the phone fold, the /why-me mini card and the Spanish footer. The cards in it respond to hover. Two decisions are not obvious from looking: the footer stays at the bottom, not under the stance, so it doesn't split the words from the artwork (the chip covers the top instead), and the mini cards skip the footer because their whole content is already the action list.

Phases, PRs, and Spikes

Three PRs, no phases and no Spike. Each is one idea, and the app is coherent at every boundary: after PR 1 the cards are single links with the new look; after PR 2 they have the same look and more targets; after PR 3 they also report clicks. PR 1 goes first because it is what was asked for and the lowest-risk change, and it shouldn't wait on a markup restructure. PR 3 goes last so the event describes the final link structure and doesn't have to change when PR 2 lands. PR 2 and PR 3 could be reviewed together if that is easier; separate, each stays small enough to read in one sitting.

Open questions & risks

Q1: Should the footer strip be solid primary or a pale tint that turns solid on hover?✓ Answered

Solid is unmistakable and echoes the blue hero above the grid; tinted is calmer beside six colourful illustrations. This is the one purely visual call in the plan, and the mockup shows both. It matters because the whole point is that the cards must be obviously clickable, and a tint is a weaker signifier at a glance.

My Recommendation
Solid primary (13.1:1 with white text). If the six blue strips feel heavy in the real page, fall back to the tint (primary on #d5e0ff is 9.9:1), which is a one-class change.
Alternatives to Consider
Tinted at rest, solid on hover and press. Or solid only on the first card in a row, which I would avoid: unequal cards are exactly what the row alignment is trying to prevent.
Answer
Solid primary, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026.

Q2: What should the footer say, in English and in Spanish?✓ Answered

Words matter more than the arrow, and the site is careful with its vocabulary (it says “action”, never “promise”). The platform page's own intro calls these “guides”, while the sheet says “Read the policy”. The Spanish label has to fit the narrowest three-column card, about 293px at 1024px. I can write Spanish, but this is campaign copy read by voters, and I shouldn't be the only reviewer.

My Recommendation
“Read the full guide” and “Leer la guía completa”. It matches the intro's word, tells a reader there is more than the card shows, and fits at that width (see the last mockup). Have a Spanish speaker on the campaign confirm the wording before merge.
Alternatives to Consider
“See the reasoning” (says what the guide adds, but is vaguer about what happens on tap). “See the full plan” (friendlier, but “plan” blurs with the site's use of “actions”). A generic “Learn more” is what this label is meant to improve on.
Answer
“Read the full guide” and “Leer la guía completa”, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. The Spanish speaker check is now a dependency of PR 1 of 3.

Q3: Should the link's accessible name become “Title, Read the full guide” instead of just the title?✓ Answered

The link's aria-label is the bare title today, so that screen readers don't recite the whole card. Now the card shows a visible label, and WCAG 2.5.3 (Label in Name) says the accessible name should contain visible label text, so that a voice-control user who says “click read the full guide” gets the card they mean. Leaving the bare title breaks that for every card.

My Recommendation
${title}, ${cta}, built by guideCardLabel. It matches the mini card's existing ${title}, ${count} shape and keeps the announcement short. The mini card's label stays as it is, since it gets no footer.
Alternatives to Consider
Drop aria-label and let the full content name the link. It satisfies 2.5.3 trivially but reads the stance and every action aloud on each card, and is the verbosity the label was added to avoid.
Answer
${title}, ${cta}, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. With rows now links (PR 2), the label sits on the heading link rather than on the whole card.

Q4: Should the /why-me mini cards get the footer strip too, or only the chip and depth?✓ Answered

They link to the same guides with the same faint border, so they have the same problem. But their content is the action list, in a compact card, and a footer would add about 3.5rem to each.

My Recommendation
Chip and depth only. The compact card keeps its density, and the chip plus tinted edge is enough to break the “finished summary” look there.
Alternatives to Consider
Same footer as the big card, for strict consistency. Reasonable if the two are ever seen side by side; today they sit on different pages.
Answer
Chip and depth only, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. Their action rows still become links in PR 2 (Q9).

Q5: Should we pull a click-through baseline from PostHog before this merges?✓ Answered

“Not everyone knows they can click” is a real observation, but I can't tell from the code how many visitors it costs. platform_guide_viewed fires on every guide page, and PostHog autocapture is on by default (the init in posthog.astro doesn't disable it), so guide views per index view is measurable today without new code. Without a before-number, a later “did it help?” can only be guessed at. I don't know this site's traffic; if it is low, an A/B test would not reach significance on a campaign timeline.

My Recommendation
Take the last 30 days of index-to-guide rate for /platform and the home page section now (about ten minutes in PostHog), merge the PR, and compare after two to four weeks. No A/B split.
Alternatives to Consider
Skip the baseline and judge by feedback alone. Or add a dedicated click event first (F3) so each of the six surfaces can be told apart.
Answer
Skip the baseline, and add a dedicated click event for the surfaces instead. Chosen in chat, 21 Sept 2026. That event is F3, now PR 3 of 3. Q10 covers what it counts.

Q6: Should other card-like links on the site get this treatment in the same change?✓ Answered

Only the two components above use the raised-card classes, so they are the whole scope of the reported problem. I did not audit the rest of the site (event listings, blog posts, the related-guides links at the foot of a guide page) for the same “doesn't look clickable” issue, and I'd rather say so than assume they are fine.

My Recommendation
No. Ship the platform cards, which are the reported problem and the highest-traffic path, and treat any other surface as its own small proposal if someone reports it.
Alternatives to Consider
A quick audit pass of the rest of the site first, if there's a suspicion others look the same.
Answer
No, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026.

Q7: When someone taps an action row, should the guide page open with that policy's sheet already open, or scroll to the policy's band with the sheet closed?✓ Answered

Rows now lead somewhere specific, so what the reader sees on arrival is a real decision. Opening the sheet uses existing behaviour: a #plank-<slug> hash opens the panel on load and fires plank_detail_opened, and the anchor is guaranteed to exist (see the Deep links finding). The cost is that the sheet covers the page, and the reader hasn't yet seen the band's headline statistic or quote until they close it.

My Recommendation
Open the sheet. The card row already showed the commitment as a headline, so the sheet's reasoning and sources are the natural next step, and it needs no new code or anchors.
Alternatives to Consider
Scroll to the band with the sheet closed, so the reader sees the statistic first and chooses to read on. That needs a second anchor scheme on the bands and gives up the plank_detail_opened signal for these arrivals.
Answer
Open the sheet on arrival, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. It uses the existing #plank-<slug> handling, so PR 2 of 3 needs no new anchors.

Q8: Should each action row show a chevron at rest, or only a tint and underline on hover?✓ Answered

The problem being fixed is that people don't know things are clickable, and a hover-only cue is invisible on a phone. A chevron on every row adds 18 small icons across the index and makes each card busier.

My Recommendation
Chevron at rest, as in the mockup. Discoverability on touch is the whole reason for this change, and a small muted chevron is the least noisy cue that works without hover.
Alternatives to Consider
Hover-only tint and underline, with no chevron, if the chevrons make the cards feel cluttered once real. Rows would then rely on the card's other cues to suggest they are tappable.
Answer
Chevron at rest on every row, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026.

Q9: Should the action rows on the /why-me mini cards be links too?✓ Answered

Q4 kept the mini cards' visuals small (chip and depth, no footer), but F2 brought rows in scope without saying which cards. A reader who learns on /platform that rows are links would reasonably expect the same on /why-me, whose rows are the entire content. The rows there are small text lines about 20 to 36px tall, so they need padding to be comfortable touch targets, which makes each card slightly taller.

My Recommendation
Yes, the same structure and behaviour as the big card, with about 6px of extra vertical padding per row. One behaviour to learn across the site is worth a slightly taller card.
Alternatives to Consider
Leave mini-card rows as plain text and link only the card. It keeps the compact card compact, at the cost of the two cards behaving differently.
Answer
Yes: the mini-card rows are links, with the same structure and about 6px of extra vertical padding per row, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. PR 2 of 3 covers both card components.

Q10: Should guide_card_clicked also cover the /why-me cards and open-in-new-tab clicks?✓ Answered

You asked for a click event on “each of these six surfaces”. The guide cards live on six routes: /platform, / and /issues, each in English and Spanish. F3, which you also brought into scope, named /why-me as well, and it adds two more routes, eight in all. I read “six” as the guide-card routes and kept why-me because F3 listed it; if you meant to leave it out, that is the answer to this question. Separately, a plain click listener misses middle-click and ctrl-click, which is how someone opens a card in a new tab.

My Recommendation
Include why-me (its surface value is why-me, so it can be filtered out in PostHog at no cost) and count auxclick as well as click. A card opened in a new tab is still a card that worked.
Alternatives to Consider
Exclude why-me if the six are all that matter, or count plain clicks only for simplicity. The latter under-counts people who open guides in tabs.
Answer
Include /why-me and count auxclick as well as click, per the recommendation. Confirmed in chat, 21 Sept 2026. So /why-me stays in: eight routes in all, and PR 3 of 3 sends the event from each.
